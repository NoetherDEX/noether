## How Lighter works (mental model)

**Architecture.** A zk-rollup on Ethereum purpose-built as an exchange: a Sequencer executes FIFO with instant soft finality; custom SNARK circuits prove *matching itself* (price-time priority encoded in an Order Book Tree; a mis-match is unprovable), aggregated into one PLONK proof verified on Ethereum before state updates. Account state is reconstructable from compressed blobs (hybrid DA). Censorship resistance = an on-chain priority queue (withdrawals, pool exits, reduce-only IOC closes) with a hard processing deadline; a missed deadline auto-freezes the whole exchange into **Escape Hatch** mode where every user self-withdraws — balances, open-position PnL at last mark, pool shares — from blob data alone ("desert exit," independently audited).

**Money model.** Retail trades at literally 0/0 fees at 300ms; monetization is opt-in Premium (0.4/2.8 bps base, 30% max discount + latency down to 140ms via 8 LIT-staking tiers), fee-credit rentals (proceeds to stakers), and buybacks. Liquidity is paid three ways: LP program (randomized ~1-min snapshots of resting depth), 50K/week MM points, and LLP itself.

**Risk engine.** Per-market three-tier margin ladder (IMR/MMR/CMR at fixed ratios 1 : 0.6 : 0.4) across ~98 markets from 50x BTC/ETH (2%/1.2%/0.8%) to 3x long-tail (33.33%/20%/13.33%). Four account states: Healthy → Pre-Liquidation (reduce-only) → Partial → Full. Partial liquidation sends IoC orders at the **zero price** — the price at which the fill keeps AccountValue/MMR exactly constant (ZP_long = mark × (1 − M·AV/MMR)) — and stops the moment the account is healthy; liquidation fee up to 1%, charged only on price improvement, routed to LLP. Full liquidation (below CMR) = LLP takes the positions over, in ascending-uPnL order, gated so LLP itself stays above its own IMR. **ADL** fires only when an account is bankrupt (negative value) AND LLP lacks capital: opposite-side accounts ranked by leverage × uPnL are force-closed where the two zero prices align, so ADL provably never worsens any account's health. LLP doubles as insurance fund and protocol counterparty; LLP Strategies shard its collateral per asset class ($1M allocation = $1M max loss; depletion ADLs only that bucket's markets); prelaunch markets get a segregated XLP pool with LLP at zero exposure.

**Pricing.** Index = Chainlink + Stork + Pyth blend. MarkPrice = **Median(ImpactPrice, index + EMA_8min(premium clamped to ±0.5% of index), median of CEX marks)** — one wick or one bad vendor print gets voted out; sustained pressure bleeds in over 8 minutes, capped at 0.5%. Funding: hourly, P2P, premium sampled 60×/hour at randomized instants, rate = clamp logic with SmallClamp 5 bps → InterestRate 1bp/8h baseline, BigClamp 4%/8h (max 0.5%/h), ÷8 spreading.

**Margin/collateral.** USDC cross by default; isolated = AllocatedMargin bucket in the same TAV framework. TAV = Collateral + uPnL; multi-asset margin (UTA-gated, ETH first, 2000 ETH global cap) via a three-parameter haircut ladder LTV ≤ LT ≤ LF (open / liquidation-trigger / seizure-floor) with dual TAV/TALT valuation. Order margin, not prefunding: resting orders cost orderValue/leverage, opposite-side orders get a 2×|position|×mark close credit; match-time health checks auto-cancel unsupportable orders.

**Order surface.** Market/limit (Post-Only, GTT/IOC), SL/TP market+limit variants (mark-price triggers), reduce-only, TWAP (30s slices) + Advanced TWAP, Chase Limit, multi-market Atomic Orders, 4 STP modes, tied child orders (bracket) inside proven execution, scheduled cancel-all (dead-man's switch) in the account leaf, 256 API keys per account and sub-account, perpRFQ block trading (side-blind, 10s MM quotes). Fat-finger collar: 5% band vs mark on aggressive placement.

**RWAs.** ~67 markets (commodities, FX, US/KR/HK equities, indices, pre-IPO) trading 24/7: oracle-primary with EMA-weight decay to internal book pricing when the underlying closes (τ = 1 min), ±1/L clamp on off-hours moves (removed on 31 liquid names 2026-07-10), 5-day 20%/day commodity rolls, synthetic-spot de-premiumed indices with a public `syntheticSpotInfo` config endpoint, absolute OI caps $10M–$200M per market, pre-IPO isolated-only with internal pricing.

**Security posture.** 9 published audit PDFs (contracts + zk circuits, ~monthly cadence), VDP with PGP intake and safe harbor, no standing bounty (discretionary rewards), sybil policy tolerating 10 accounts/user.

## Where Noether already matches or beats Lighter (parity highlights)

The full parity inventory (81 rows) lives in the dimension sections; the load-bearing ones:

- **Every fill is zero-slippage at oracle price by construction** — the property Lighter engineers with zero-price bounds and median marking falls out of the pool model (conditional on oracle quality). perpRFQ and STP are structurally unnecessary; fat-finger protection comes free.
- **Everything-on-chain with ~5s L1 hard finality** — no soft-finality trust window, no pre-proof feed gap (Lighter's own whitepaper admits its feed pre-commitment scheme is future work). Noether's counter-narrative: *"we don't prove execution followed the rules; execution IS the rules."*
- **Trader-side escape hatch mostly exists by construction**: closes are never staleness/deviation-blocked (M-2 lenient path), settlement works while paused, liquidations permissionless, router `close_with_price` lets anyone self-relay a fresh price. Lighter needed an L1 priority queue + freeze mode to approximate this. (Residuals: pause asymmetry P0, publication P1 — see amendments.)
- **Open-time full-notional reservation** (70% AUM aggregate / 25% per-asset-side, error #82): the pool can always physically pay the max payout of every open position — a hard solvency guarantee Lighter's LLP depositors don't get.
- **Partial liquidation live on staging** (isolated: 20% tranches, 30s cooldown, bankruptcy override) — same graduated philosophy as Lighter's MMR→CMR band.
- **Insurance buffer live on staging** correctly excluded from LP NAV, fed 10% of all liquidation proceeds, paying winners before LP capital.
- **Fail-safe oracle handling in margin math**: failed read ⇒ not liquidatable (i128::MAX/2 sentinel) and withdrawals price unknown marks at 0 — conservative in both directions.
- **Non-custodial API**: `/v1/orders/prepare` returns unsigned txs bound to the authenticated owner — a leaked Noether key can't trade or move funds; a leaked Lighter key trades live.
- **Volume fee tiers cut deeper than Lighter's staking ladder** (75% off at tier 3 vs 30% max), 100% of fees to LPs, no pay-for-latency asymmetry possible on Soroban.
- **Published bug bounty bands beat Lighter's no-standing-bounty stance** (SECURITY.md: up to 10% of funds at risk, capped $10–25k, 72h ack, SEAL 911).
- **Trailing stops on-chain** — not in Lighter's documented order set at all.
- **NOE LP shares are real wallet assets** (SDEX-tradeable); Lighter pool shares have no documented secondary market.
- **Geo-compliance machinery code-complete** (edge rewrites + gateway 451 + ToS warranties) — absent from Lighter's docs.

## Risk engine — liquidations, insurance fund, ADL

## [P0/L] Auto-Deleveraging (ADL): no executable mechanism — the pool has no last-resort de-risking step between insurance depletion and short-paying winners
**Lighter:** ADL fires only when an account's value is NEGATIVE (bankrupt, still holding >=1 position) AND the LLP lacks capital to cover the loss. Counterparties are opposite-side positions ranked by leverage and unrealized PnL (most profitable / most levered deleveraged first). Forced fills execute only where the two sides' zero prices align — zeroPrice_long = mark x (1 - M_i x AV/MMR), zeroPrice_short = mark x (1 + M_i x AV/MMR) — so the execution price can never be worse than either side's bankruptcy-neutral bound and ADL provably never decreases ANY account's health. ADL is fully backstopped by LLP depositors; LLP Strategies shard collateral per asset class so depleting e.g. the RWA bucket ADLs only that bucket's markets. Prelaunch markets go straight to ADL below CMR with the counterparty compensated at a better-than-mark price.
**Noether:** missing (execution). Ranking math exists unit-tested in the UNDEPLOYED contracts/risk crate: adl_rank = PnL% x leverage, losers dropped (contracts/risk/src/risk.rs:181-191, lib.rs:146-176) — but the risk contract is absent from contracts.json, the keeper has zero ADL wiring, and the market has NO force-close entrypoint against profitable positions (only trader-auth close_position and keeper liquidate of underwater ones, contracts/market/src/lib.rs:471-674). Live waterfall is margin -> buffer -> LP -> unpayable Shortfall; the intended mainnet waterfall margin -> buffer -> ADL -> LP (TASKS.md:185, param sheet ~232-239) has a hole where ADL should be.
**Why:** In the pool model the vault is the counterparty to every winner, so winners' claims are uncapped against LP capital: in a one-sided crash, aggregate winner uPnL grows while payable AUM shrinks, and the terminal behavior today is settle_pnl short-paying whoever closes last (Shortfall, never repaid). That is socialized loss assigned by race — invisible until the day it happens, then fatal to trust. Every serious perp venue (Lighter, Binance, Hyperliquid post-JELLY) has a deterministic answer to 'the backstop is empty'; Noether currently has an IOU counter.
**Build:** Translate, don't port: in a pool model the 'opposite side' of every bankrupt loser is the vault itself, so ADL = force-realizing the highest-ranked WINNERS at the current oracle mark to cap pool liability (they are paid IN FULL and lose only future upside — the pool-model equivalent of Lighter's 'never worse than zero price' guarantee, since oracle-priced closes have no slippage). Build: (1) market entrypoint adl_close(caller, position_id) — permissionless, valid only while an AdlActive(asset) flag is set; (2) permissionless check_adl_trigger(asset) flips the flag when payable coverage (vault BufferBalance + total_usdc) < ~1.25x aggregate positive trader uPnL for that asset (computable from the per-asset exposure aggregates the market already pushes via sync_exposure), or immediately on any payout_shortfall event; (3) on-chain gate checks only trigger-active + uPnL > 0 + asset match — execution ORDER comes from the keeper walking adl_rank (deploy contracts/risk or inline the pure function in the keeper; ranking is advisory, the gate is consensus); (4) settle through the existing close path (cross positions credit the cross pool), optionally paying adl_compensation_bps from the buffer like Lighter's prelaunch better-than-mark compensation; (5) emit adl_executed(position_id, trader, asset, size, price, score), decode in indexer, expose kind:adl in /v1/trades, push on account.events WS; (6) UI: Binance-style ADL-queue quintile per position computed by the gateway from indexer positions + marks so users see their rank BEFORE it fires. Explicitly prefer ADL over pro-rata winner haircuts (socialization) — deterministic, targeted, industry-standard.
**Where:** contracts/market/src/lib.rs (adl_close + AdlActive trigger), contracts/risk (deploy) or scripts/keeper/src (inline ranking), indexer/src/decoders+handlers/market.ts, api/src/routes/trades.ts + positions route, web/components/trading/PositionsList.tsx

## [P0/M] Full liquidation confiscates 100% of remaining equity (isolated and cross) instead of charging a bounded penalty and returning the residual
**Lighter:** Liquidation is minimum-intervention: IoC fills bounded by the zero price mean a liquidated trader 'often retains residual equity'; the liquidation fee is 'up to 1%', charged ONLY when the fill beats the zero price, and routed to the LLP insurance fund. Even full liquidation (below CMR = 0.4 x IMR) is an LLP position takeover, not confiscation of residual account value. Prelaunch markets charge ZERO liquidation fee.
**Noether:** implemented but confiscatory (verified in code): isolated full liquidation pays the keeper 5% of remaining equity (cap 10% of collateral) then transfers ALL remaining collateral to the vault, split 90% LP / 10% insurance buffer (contracts/market/src/lib.rs:471-674); liquidate_cross_account sends remaining positive trader equity to the VAULT with comment 'Send remaining trader equity to vault' and zeroes the cross balance (lib.rs:1097-1112). TASKS.md mainnet sheet already targets a 1%-of-notional penalty split 50/50 keeper/insurance — not implemented. Partial liquidation softens this on isolated (staging only; prod contracts from 2026-07-06 predate it) but the full-liq terminal step still seizes everything.
**Why:** A trader one tick under the 1% MM loses ~99% of a 10x position's remaining margin where Lighter charges <=1% and returns the rest. On mainnet with real money this reads as theft, generates the loudest possible complaints ('I had $400 equity left and got zero back'), and makes every liquidation a churn event instead of a survivable one.
**Build:** Change full-liquidation settlement to: penalty = liquidation_penalty_bps x notional (adopt the TASKS sheet's 100 bps, split 50/50 keeper/insurance-buffer), transfer only realized loss + funding + penalty to the vault, and RETURN remaining = collateral + pnl - funding - penalty to the trader (isolated) or back to the cross pool (cross). Bankruptcy (remaining <= 0) unchanged. Ship in the coupled T3 market+vault redeploy with regression tests alongside test_liquidation_reward_capped; keeps the keeper economics positive on every non-bankrupt liquidation.
**Where:** contracts/market/src/lib.rs:471-674 and 980-1132, contracts/noether_common/src/types.rs (penalty config), contract tests; events already carry amounts so indexer/web changes are minor

## [P0/M] Insurance fund never covers bad debt, and bad debt is completely unbooked — no draw path, no ledger, no event, no metric
**Lighter:** The LLP is the explicit bad-debt buyer: below close-out it absorbs whole accounts in ascending-uPnL order subject to a formal solvency gate (post-takeover LLP value must stay above LLP's own Initial Margin Requirement), it covers bankrupt accounts' losses, and only when IT lacks capital does ADL fire. Loss capacity is explicit and sharded per LLP Strategy (a $1M allocation = $1M max loss exposure, depletion contained to that strategy's markets).
**Noether:** partial. The buffer's ONLY spend path is paying winner PnL before LP capital (contracts/vault/src/lib.rs:321-384) — it is NEVER tapped for negative-equity liquidations. Bankrupt losses bypass it: isolated outflows cap at the position's own collateral (lib.rs:2158-2174) and cross losses cap at the market contract's USDC balance with the excess literally commented 'bad debt absorbed by vault' (lib.rs:1061-1077). No bad_debt event, no counter, nothing indexed; the /vault page shows an 'Insurance Fund' card that does not insure the loss side.
**Why:** LPs eat every bankruptcy silently through NAV with zero disclosure — they cannot price the risk they're taking, the team cannot monitor backstop health, and the first visible NAV gap-down with no explanation triggers LP flight. It also inverts the label: users reasonably assume 'insurance fund' means bad-debt cover; discovering it doesn't is a trust hit on top of the loss.
**Build:** Make the waterfall real: on any liquidation where remaining = collateral + pnl - funding < 0, book bad_debt = -remaining explicitly — (1) add vault.draw_buffer(amount) (market-only, mirror of fund_buffer) that covers bad debt from BufferBalance first, remainder debited from LP accounting via an explicit path; (2) emit bad_debt_recorded(trader, asset, amount, buffer_covered, lp_absorbed) from the market; (3) index it and publish cumulative bad debt + current buffer balance on /v1/markets/stats and the vault page. This turns the buffer into an actual insurance fund (margin -> buffer -> ADL -> LP) rather than only a winner-payout smoother, and gives LPs the disclosure Lighter gives via published waterfall rules.
**Where:** contracts/vault/src/lib.rs (draw_buffer + event), contracts/market/src/lib.rs liquidation settle paths (lib.rs:509-516, 624-642, 1016-1077), indexer/src/handlers/market.ts, api/src/services/stats.ts, web/components/vault/StatsBar.tsx

## [P0/M] Shortfall is an unpayable IOU — winners short-paid at close are never made whole, even after the buffer refills
**Lighter:** Lighter's design has no short-pay state: partial liquidations fill against real book liquidity, full liquidation is a solvency-gated LLP takeover, and when LLP can't cover, ADL closes the winner at a zero-price-bounded fill — a winner is deleveraged at a fair bound, never stiffed. There is no IOU counter anywhere in the waterfall.
**Noether:** missing repayment (verified in code): settle_pnl caps the payout at what the pool physically holds and records the gap via set_shortfall(get_shortfall + short) — the ONLY write, an increment (contracts/vault/src/lib.rs:366) — plus a payout_shortfall event; get_shortfall (lib.rs:730-732) is view-only. ## [P1/S] Single maintenance threshold does triple duty — no initial-margin regime or pre-liquidation de-risk band

## [P0/S] Stop-loss guaranteed execution (cancel-on-slippage-breach abandons users in fast markets)
**Lighter:** Plain SL/TP fire as market-style orders — execution is guaranteed once mark crosses the trigger; only the explicit SL-limit/TP-limit variants trade fill certainty for price control, and that trade-off is the user's choice. - Core trigger-order suite live on-chain — limit entry, two-phase stop-limit, stop-loss, take-profit, take-limit — matching Lighter's SL/TP market+limit coverage (contracts/market/src/lib.rs:1175-1901).
- Trailing stop exists on-chain with keeper-maintained peaks (place_trailing_stop, lib.rs:1905-2011) — not present in Lighter's documented order set.
- Reduce-only can never flip or grow a position (M-6 fix: executes only against an opposing position, refunds collateral, lib.rs:2408-2451) — the same core guarantee as Lighter's reduce-only flag.
- Mandatory per-order slippage tolerance (1-10000 bps, 0 rejected #66) on every trigger order — Lighter's market orders carry no slippage parameter at all ('slippage unbounded by the order itself').
- OCO-at-close: executing SL/TP/liquidation auto-cancels sibling attached orders via cancel_position_orders (lib.rs:2280, 663, 962, 1056, 2194) — equivalent of Lighter's tied child-order cleanup on position sign change.
- Atomic verify-then-trade: market opens/closes execute in the user's own signed tx at a just-relayed attested oracle price with immediate L1 finality (noether_router lib.rs:172-305) — no sequencer soft-finality trust window to worry about.
- perpRFQ not needed: the firm-price-for-size guarantee Lighter provides whales via whitelisted RFQ (10s MM quotes, 2-min execution window, side-blind) is native to the pool model — every fill is zero-price-impact at oracle price up to the $100k position and OI caps; capacity, not price impact, is the binding constraint.
- Self-trade prevention is structurally not applicable: there is no resting maker liquidity and the counterparty to every fill is the LP vault, so the accidental self-crossing Lighter needs 4 STP modes for cannot occur.
- Placement-time margin certainty: entry orders prefund 100% of collateral at placement (lib.rs:1175-1321), so a fill can never breach margin at execution — the guarantee Lighter engineers via order-margin accounting plus match-time auto-cancel.
- Fat-finger protection by construction: fills only ever price at oracle, and the slippage-vs-reference check auto-cancels absurdly-priced trigger orders at execution (lib.rs:1671-1721) — same user outcome as Lighter's 5% placement collar, different mechanism.
- Triggers evaluate on the oracle mark, not venue last-trade — the same 'mark, not last print' design choice as Lighter (the missing smoothing/median layer is tracked as a P0 gap).
- Non-custodial programmatic order flow with owner binding: /v1/orders/prepare hard-binds trader = authenticated key owner and the gateway never holds keys (api/src/routes/orders.ts:317-322) — a stricter custody posture than any bearer-key CEX-style API.

## [P0/L] Smoothed, manipulation-resistant liquidation mark (TWAP/median vs one raw print)
**Lighter:** MarkPrice = Median(ImpactPrice, price1, price2) where price1 = index + EMA_8min(clamp(ImpactPrice − index, ±index/200)) and price2 = median of CEX mark prices. The own-book premium contribution is hard-capped at ±0.5% of index and smoothed over 8 minutes, so a manipulator must hold a distortion for minutes and can never push mark >0.5% past the index-anchored components; liquidations and SL/TP triggers evaluate on this mark, never on last trade.
**Noether:** MISSING. Liquidation margin checks, stored liq-price triggers, SL/TP/trailing triggers, PnL, and vault NAV all read one instantaneous Noeracle print (contracts/market/src/lib.rs:2042-2081, verified). Worse: closes/liquidations pass strict=false, so NEITHER the 60s staleness bound NOR the 1% deviation band applies on the liquidation path — on-chain, the liquidation price is completely ungated. No EMA/median/TWAP exists anywhere (AUDIT O-4 open; P2-2 Noeracle ring buffer + twap not started; P5-8 blocked on it). The keeper also bypasses router sanity bands by calling market.liquidate directly (scripts/keeper/src/stellar.ts:437-450).
**Why:** One bad round — a publisher bug, an upstream source glitch, or a spoofed write until O-1 prod cutover — liquidates healthy positions at a price nobody could trade at, and the contract accepts it unconditionally. 'Liquidated by a scam wick' is the most reputation-fatal event class for a perp venue; Lighter spends three independent components plus an EMA to prevent exactly this.
**Build:** Trigger on smoothed, settle on fresh: (1) ship the P2-2 Noeracle ring buffer prices(asset,n) + twap(asset,n); (2) in the market's lenient path, make liquidation ELIGIBILITY require breach on both fresh spot AND a 1–3 min TWAP/median (or two consecutive rounds), while settling the fill at the fresh attested spot relayed via router liquidate_with_price; (3) evaluate SL/stop triggers against the same smoothed reference. Preserve halt-open/allow-close — smoothing must add persistence requirements, never block a genuinely underwater liquidation (fall back to spot-only if the ring buffer read fails).
**Where:** Noeracle repo (ring buffer + TWAP view), contracts/noeracle_shim (expose twap), contracts/market/src/lib.rs get_oracle_price + liquidate/execute paths, scripts/keeper

# Appendix B — Lighter whitepaper internals (23 pages)

## Verifiable matching engine (matching proved inside SNARK circuits, not at the sequencer)
Lighter's core claim: the matching and liquidation engines run INSIDE the Lighter Prover's custom arithmetic circuits, not merely at the Sequencer. The Prover generates SNARK execution proofs for complete financial operations — price-time-priority order matching, risk management, liquidations, and account updates. The Sequencer only determines transaction ORDER; since execution and matching are verified by the Prover, all valid state transitions are deterministically inferred from the transaction order plus oracle data. The paper explicitly contrasts this with existing zk-rollup order-book architectures where matching occurs at the Sequencer layer and proofs 'merely confirm that two orders intersect' — there the sequencer can favor certain maker orders, deprioritize others, or manipulate matching outcomes, especially in low-volatility/narrow-spread markets with multiple makers at identical price levels. Lighter enforces time-based prioritization at the proof level. The circuits are stateless: they verify the consistency of supplied witnesses (Merkle paths, leaves) against the state root rather than holding state. Footnote 2: Lighter does not currently operate as a privacy-focused rollup — zk-SNARKs are used for efficient verification of execution correctness, not privacy.
PARAMS: No numeric parameters; the guarantee is structural: 'Without enforced time-based prioritization at the proof level, the Sequencer could arbitrarily reorder or favor trades.' Proof system: custom arithmetic circuits + multi-layered aggregation into a single succinct proof, final proof wrapped in PLONK and verified on Ethereum; Poseidon2 hash for merkleization; commitment (composite of state roots + tx/blob data) is the single public input.

## Protocol components (Sequencer / Prover / Witness / Indexer / API Servers / Smart Contracts)
Sequencer: low-latency execution engine; consumes user txs from the mempool, executes sequentially, structures them into blocks, aggregates blocks into batches, submits batches to the Ethereum smart contracts as state update proposals; broadcasts receipts and state changes via data feeds to the Indexer and Prover; integrates with decentralized oracle networks for index prices. It distributes batch data to parallelized Witness services which produce the inputs consumed by the Prover. Indexer: digests the Sequencer feed into a user-friendly structure. API Servers: serve indexed data, accept tx submissions, primary interface (REST/WebSocket). Prover: consumes the execution feed and generates succinct proofs for all exchange operations taking (previous state, batch of user txs) as inputs. Smart Contracts on Ethereum: hold deposited assets and the canonical Lighter state root; store batch commitments; on successful proof verification they update the state root and execute on-chain messages (e.g., withdrawals). A state update proposal contains: new state root, data marking queued priority requests as processed, Lighter-to-Ethereum messages (withdrawals), and blob data (account-level state updates + global market info such as funding rates and mark prices). A pre-commitment scheme for the low-latency feeds 'will be introduced' so broadcast data provably corresponds to subsequently committed on-chain state (rapid cryptographic finality) — i.e., it does not exist yet.
PARAMS: Throughput claim: 'Lighter executes tens of thousands of user operations per second.' Sequencer queue discipline: 'first-come, first-served.' Batch commits to Ethereum happen 'frequently' (no number). Block commitment = composite hash of old and new state roots combined with block and transaction data.

## Batch data hierarchy and hybrid data availability
Hierarchy (Figure 1): Transactions (user- or system-initiated unit ops: create order, withdraw, initiate liquidation) -> Block = one Block Pre-Execution (global exchange ops: applying oracle inputs, premium calculations across perp markets) + a sequence of consecutive transactions -> Segment = multiple consecutive blocks -> Batch = consecutive segments + attached blob data. Batches form a linear chain, genesis height 0, each batch height +1. Because posting everything on Ethereum is impossible at this volume, Lighter uses a hybrid DA model: it posts ONLY the minimal data required for a secure Escape Hatch. Since high-frequency trading is dominated by order operations that rarely result in trades, order placements/cancels that do not change account values are omitted from L1 data. Lighter aggregates all account state changes that affect users' total account values across the batch into a Merkle tree, serializes the non-empty leaves into compressed byte data ('Account Blob Data Segments'), combines it with market data (funding rates and mark prices for all perpetual markets), and publishes it to Ethereum in a blob-carrying transaction. The proofs attest both correctness of the state update AND 'the validity and completeness of the accompanying on-chain data — demonstrating that the information within Ethereum's data blobs is sufficient to fully reconstruct all user states using only censorship-resistant on-chain data.'
PARAMS: Genesis batch height = 0; batch height increments by 1; no branching. Blob content per batch: non-empty Public-Account-Tree deltas + funding rates + mark prices for all perp markets. No batch frequency or blob size numbers given.

## Rollup vs Priority transactions (censorship resistance)
Two queuing mechanisms. (1) Rollup transactions: all exchange operations after deposit; queued through the API Servers, consumed directly by the Sequencer; 'do not incur any transaction fees.' (2) Priority transactions: censorship-resistant exit operations — closing positions, withdrawing assets back to Ethereum, redeeming shares from public pools — queued DIRECTLY through the Ethereum smart contracts. The contracts mandate that the Sequencer include queued priority transactions in committed blocks within a predefined/specified time frame. If the Sequencer omits any queued priority transaction past the deadline, the contracts activate the Escape Hatch: freeze the exchange state, halt all further block commitments and verifications, and allow direct withdrawals. The priority framework is explicitly extensible ('future integration of additional priority operations, serving as a composability interface between Lighter and the broader Ethereum ecosystem').
PARAMS: Rollup tx fee = 0 ('do not incur any transaction fees'). Priority-tx inclusion deadline = 'a predefined time frame' (no number in the paper).

## State Tree (global merkleized state)

## Order nonce scheme (time priority without timestamps)
Instead of storing creation timestamps, each order gets a nonce — an integer used to compare relative creation times. Nonce values for ask and bid orders start from zero independently (per side, per market), and each new order's nonce is incremented by one from the previous order on the same side in the same market. O (nonce bit-width) is chosen large enough that (a) ask orders always have smaller nonce values than INVERTED bid order nonces, and (b) the total nonce range does not exceed 2^O - 1; the data structure permits order insertions only while these nonce conditions hold (i.e., ask_nonce_max + bid_nonce_max < 2^O - 1 keeps the two sides' leaf ranges disjoint at any price).
PARAMS: Nonces start at 0, increment by 1 per new order per side per market. Inverse of a nonce (footnote 4): 'the value obtained by inverting all of its O bits' = 2^O - 1 - o. Total nonce range must not exceed 2^O - 1.

## Order Book Tree leaf indexing (price-time priority encoded in the leaf index)
Active orders are stored in leaves of the Order Book Tree, a prefix-tree-like structure of height H = O + P where the top P bits of the leaf index are the price and the bottom O bits are the (possibly inverted) nonce. Ask order with price p and nonce o: index I = p x 2^O + o. Bid order: I = p x 2^O + (2^O - 1 - o) (inverted nonce). This yields total ordering theorems: for two ask orders, index_i < index_j if and only if (price_i < price_j) OR (price_i = price_j AND nonce_i < nonce_j) — so for asks, SMALLER index = higher priority (lower price, then earlier). For two bid orders, index_i > index_j if and only if (price_i > price_j) OR (price_i = price_j AND nonce_i < nonce_j) — so for bids, LARGER index = higher priority. The book invariant max{price of any bid} < min{price of any ask} is maintained (crossing orders cannot coexist — they would have matched). Consequently bids occupy the left side of the tree with the best bid at the rightmost leaf of that group, and asks occupy the right side with the best ask at the leftmost ask leaf. Priority comparison reduces to integer comparison of leaf indexes, which is cheap in circuits.
PARAMS: Ask: I = p x 2^O + o. Bid: I = p x 2^O + (2^O - 1 - o). Tree height H = O + P (P = price bits, O = nonce bits). Worked example in the paper: H = 5, P = 2, O = 3 with sample orders (price 1 nonce 0 size 2 bid; price 2 nonce 1 size 2 bid; price 3 nonce 0 size 2 ask; price 3 nonce 1 size 5 ask).

## Order Book Tree internal node aggregates and hashing
Each internal node i stores four running aggregates over the orders in its subtree: AskSizeSum_i = sum over orders j in SubTree_i of size_j x isAsk_j (eq 1); BidSizeSum_i = sum of size_j x (1 - isAsk_j) (eq 2); AskQuoteSum_i = sum of size_j x price_j x isAsk_j (eq 3); BidQuoteSum_i = sum of size_j x price_j x (1 - isAsk_j) (eq 4). Computed recursively: each sum at node i = same sum at left child + same sum at right child (eqs 5-8); leaves use the single-order versions (eqs 9-12). Any leaf change touches only the root-to-leaf path, so updates are O(H). Hashing (eq 13): Hash_i = Hash(Order_i) if i is a leaf; Hash_i = Hash(Hash_left_i, Hash_right_i, InternalData_i) otherwise — i.e., unlike a plain Merkle tree, internal node DATA (the four sums) is part of the hash, so the aggregates are themselves committed and verifiable. Internal nodes also carry information indicating the presence of ask-order leaves in left/right subtrees, essential for verifying that a claimed best ask is truly the leftmost ask leaf. The size/quote sums additionally support computing average execution price for a specified quote size (walk the tree using the aggregates) — used to determine impact prices, which feed premium computations for funding.
PARAMS: Four aggregates per internal node: AskSizeSum, BidSizeSum, AskQuoteSum, BidQuoteSum; exact formulas eqs 1-12 as above; hash rule eq 13: Hash_i = Hash(Order_i) for leaves, Hash(Hash_left, Hash_right, InternalData_i) for internal nodes; Poseidon2 as the hash.

## Data structure complexity rationale (why the Order Book Tree)
The Prover circuits are stateless and must verify order witnesses against a single state root, so the choice of structure dominates proof cost. Alternatives analyzed for N active orders: a priority-sorted Linked List needs a composite hash over all nodes — Theta(N) hashes for existence proof, quote retrieval, and per execution cycle. An Embedded Linked List (linked list inside a Merkle tree, each leaf storing prev/next indexes) gets Theta(log2 N) existence proofs, but each insert/remove touches a leaf and its two neighbors — three leaf-to-root witness paths — and quote retrieval must prove existence of every active order: O(N log2 N). The Order Book Tree encodes priority directly in leaf indexes (inherently sorted, best ask = leftmost ask leaf, best bid = rightmost bid leaf) and stores subtree aggregates in internal nodes, achieving Theta(log2 N) for existence verification, best-ask/bid identification, insertion, removal, AND quote retrieval — all along a single root-to-leaf path per operation.
PARAMS: Table 2 verbatim: Linked List — existence Theta(N), quote retrieval Theta(N), total hashes per execution cycle Theta(N). Embedded Linked List — Theta(log2 N), O(N log2 N), O(N log2 N). Order Book Tree — Theta(log2 N), Theta(log2 N), Theta(log2 N).

## Instruction Stack (multi-cycle operations and exchange-triggered actions)
The Lighter VM's unit of work is the execution cycle; most transactions take one cycle, but some span several: a taker order producing multiple trades, 'cancel all account orders', or grouped order placement. There are also exchange-triggered events with no user transaction: canceling all user orders when a scheduled 'cancel all' (dead man's switch) fires, or canceling position-tied orders (e.g., reduce-only orders) when the sign of an account's position changes during a trade. The Instruction Stack — part of the global state — drives this: it defines the action for the current execution cycle; the next user-signed transaction executes only when the stack is empty. Contents: Instruction Type (uniquely defines the current action, e.g., inserting an order, canceling a set of orders, or triggering child orders of a parent order such as tied SL/TP order trigger events) and Instruction Details (parameters plus generic fields carrying data between consecutive cycles: related order details, account indexes, market indexes, and other continuation context).
PARAMS: None numeric. Semantics: one instruction per execution cycle; user txs blocked while stack non-empty; supports parent/child order relationships (tied stop-loss/take-profit), reduce-only auto-cancel on position sign change, scheduled cancel-all.

## Block circuits and execution cycle validation
A block = pre-execution phase + a series of execution cycles (Figure 10). After all cycles, the circuits compute a block commitment — a composite hash of the old and new state roots combined with the block and transaction data — and the Prover generates a succinct proof per block with this commitment as public input for the aggregation layer. Per execution cycle (Figure 12): if the Instruction Stack is empty, the circuits validate the new transaction — checking transaction data and confirming that the provided signature (supplied as witness) matches the transaction initiator and the LATEST API key nonce — then validate transaction details and push the instruction; if non-empty, they continue the stacked instruction. All witness data is validated by verifying each node along the provided Merkle paths against both the State Root and the Account Delta Tree; the circuits ensure all leaf data required for execution is correctly supplied (e.g., account/sub-account leaves correspond to the tx initiator; a provided order path points to the correct leaf when canceling). Risk checks confirm the account maintains sufficient margin BEFORE and AFTER execution. Then the circuits execute the transaction, update leaf data, and derive the new roots. Witness inputs enumerated: state tree leaves, state tree Merkle proofs, old/new state root, new validium root, old/new account delta tree root, account delta tree leaves + Merkle proofs; leaf kinds: Account Leaf, API Key Leaf, Account Order Leaf, Order Leaf, Perpetual Market Details, Instruction Stack.
PARAMS: Margin rule stated only as 'sufficient margin before and after execution' (no initial/maintenance margin numbers). Signature check binds to latest API key nonce (replay protection).

## Order insertion with proven no-cross check (maker path)
An order is inserted into the book if and only if there are no crossing orders on the opposite side, and the circuits PROVE this using internal node values along one root-to-leaf path — no need to touch the opposite side's leaves. For a new ask order with index I = I_0 I_1 ... I_(H-1) (bit I_i = 0 means node L_i is the left child of L_(i+1), 1 means right child), the crossing set is S = {order_i | order_i is a bid order AND index_order_i > index_order_new} (eq 14). Its total size (eq 15-16): crossingSize = sum over orders in S of size = sum over path nodes L_i in L, 0 < i <= H, of (BidSizeSum_(L_i) - BidSizeSum_(L_(i-1))) x (1 - I_(i-1)) — i.e., traverse root-to-leaf and add the right-subtree bid sizes at every node where the path continues into the left child (those right subtrees hold exactly the bids with higher index = price >= new ask's price). For a new bid order: S = {order_i | ask AND index_order_i < index_order_new} (eq 17), crossingSize = sum of (AskSizeSum_(L_i) - AskSizeSum_(L_(i-1))) x I_(i-1) (eqs 18-19) — left-subtree ask sizes where the path goes right. The circuits compute crossingSize from the witness path and confirm it equals ZERO before inserting the new order.
PARAMS: Eq 16: crossingSize = SUM_(L_i in L, 0<i<=H) (BidSizeSum_(L_i) - BidSizeSum_(L_(i-1))) x (1 - I_(i-1)). Eq 19: crossingSize = SUM_(L_i in L, 0<i<=H) (AskSizeSum_(L_i) - AskSizeSum_(L_(i-1))) x I_(i-1). Insertion requires crossingSize = 0. Cost: single root-to-leaf path, Theta(log2 N) hashes.

## Pending trade execution with proven best-maker selection (taker path)
If crossing orders exist (crossingSize > 0 for the incoming order), the circuits expect the provided witness order leaf to be the HIGHEST-PRIORITY crossing order, and verify it before executing a trade. For a taker ask order_new matching maker bid order_cross with index I: the circuits take the root-to-leaf path L to index I as witness, confirm order_cross is indeed a crossing order on the opposite side, then verify it has the highest priority among ALL crossing orders by recalculating crossingSize using path L and confirming crossingSize = 0 relative to it (i.e., no bid with index greater than I exists — nothing better-priced or earlier-at-price remains). If the condition holds, the circuits validate the pending trade — performing checks such as order expiry, reduce-only constraints, and margin sufficiency — and only then execute the trade and update the Instruction Stack (multi-fill takers loop through further cycles, each consuming the then-best maker).
PARAMS: Best-maker proof = crossingSize(path to claimed best maker) = 0 on the strict-better-priority region. Trade-time validations listed verbatim: 'order expiry, reduce-only constraints, and margin sufficiency.'

## Multi-layered proof aggregation and blob equivalence circuits
Proof pipeline (Figure 13): a Linear Tx Aggregator combines execution cycles into a block proof; a Linear Block Aggregator combines blocks into a segment proof; segments act as the parallelization layer — block proofs are generated independently, then linearly aggregated into segment proofs; a Linear Segment Aggregator combines all segments into one batch proof; a Linear Public Data Aggregator handles the public data segments; a final batch proof is wrapped by a PLONK Wrapper and submitted to Ethereum for on-chain verification. Beyond execution correctness, the Prover verifies posted blob data corresponds to execution public data in two stages: (1) Posted Blob–Claimed Blob Equivalence — Ethereum's polynomial evaluation precompile: when committing a batch, the protocol evaluates the blob polynomial at a random point and includes the point and result in the final proof commitment as public inputs; the Blob Polynomial Equivalence Circuit evaluates the claimed blob polynomial at the same point and verifies both match. (2) Execution Public Data–Claimed Blob Equivalence — Account Delta Traversal Circuits serialize the Account Delta Tree Root into a stream of values and evaluate a polynomial constructed from this stream at a random point; in parallel, batch circuits deserialize the claimed blob bytes into a value stream and perform the same evaluation; after verifying the Linear Public Data Aggregator's proof, the batch circuits compare both evaluations. Random evaluation points for both checks derive from the Fiat–Shamir heuristic, ensuring all polynomial inputs are encompassed in the randomness generation. After all batch-level verifications, a single public commitment is computed from the aggregated data and the proof is generated, PLONK-wrapped, and verified on Ethereum.
PARAMS: Final wrapper: PLONK. Randomness: Fiat–Shamir. Blob check uses Ethereum's point-evaluation precompile (EIP-4844 machinery). One succinct proof per batch regardless of transaction count.

## Escape Hatch (frozen-state trustless exit)
Normal mode offers two withdrawal paths (rollup tx via Sequencer, priority tx via contracts). If the Sequencer fails to include ANY priority transaction in a batch by the deadline, the system permissionlessly transitions into Escape Hatch mode — 'a robust fail-safe mechanism, intended never to be triggered,' aligning Sequencer incentives. Activation freezes the protocol state: all further batch commitments and verifications are disabled, halting the Sequencer; the smart contracts then only support user exit operations. Because posted blob data contains everything needed to reconstruct the complete Public Account Tree and Public Perpetual Market Details, users can independently generate proofs verifying their asset ownership, open position PnL, and public pool share values; the contracts validate these proofs against the frozen state root and release the assets. Footnote 6 (settlement rule): 'When the Escape Hatch is triggered, all open positions are assumed to be settled at the latest mark price. If an account holds open positions at the time of activation, the user can prove the details of these positions and their PnL using the publicly available blob data. They may then withdraw their unrealized PnL, the value of their public pool shares, and their remaining assets.'
PARAMS: Trigger: any queued priority tx omitted from committed blocks past the predefined deadline (deadline value not given). Settlement: all open positions settled at the LATEST mark price in the frozen state. Exit proof scope: asset ownership + open position PnL + public pool share values.

## MEV mitigation stance and residual trust
Because matching is proof-enforced, most malicious behaviors (priority alteration) are impossible, 'substantially reducing the economic incentives for MEV extraction.' What remains is the Sequencer's control over transaction ORDERING before proofs: since Lighter operates at extremely low latency, any reordering advantage 'is confined to millisecond-scale windows, making such opportunities difficult to exploit and with negligible user impact in practice.' Future work aims to eliminate residual effects: fair sequencing techniques and cryptographic methods such as transaction encryption and pre-commitment schemes, creating incentives for operators to deliver instant finality and uphold integrity 'even before proof verification is complete.' Separation of duties also enables decentralization: because the Sequencer only orders (never matches), 'parts of the Sequencer service [can] be decentralized more easily compared to existing zk-rollup architectures for order book systems.'
PARAMS: Residual MEV window: millisecond-scale. Planned mitigations (not yet live): pre-commitment scheme for data feeds, transaction encryption, fair sequencing.
